BLUESQUID REAR CONTROLLER PCB DESIGN BRIEF FOR FLUX.AI
Revision: Draft A
Date: 2026-08-20
Project repository: BlueSquid_v1

IMPORTANT STATUS
================

This document is an engineering input brief, not a fabrication-approved design.
Flux.AI should use it to create a preliminary architecture, schematic, PCB and
BOM. Ratings marked TBD must be confirmed before schematic sign-off. Do not
silently select a value or infer a safety rating for a TBD item.

The first custom PCB should be a serviceable rear-controller carrier and I/O
board. The 7-inch front touchscreen is an existing Waveshare
ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-7 assembly and must not be recreated on this PCB.

No 120 VAC conductors or switching contacts are permitted on the BlueSquid PCB.
The PCB may operate protected low-voltage contactor coils that control mains
equipment in a separate approved enclosure.


1. PROJECT PURPOSE
==================

BlueSquid is a camper-van monitoring and control system. It has:

1. A front 7-inch ESP32-S3 touchscreen near the camper door.
2. A rear ESP32-S3 controller close to the electrical equipment and loads.
3. A persistent bonded BLE communications link between the front and rear nodes.
4. Separate interfaces for Victron equipment and Dometic RV-C equipment.
5. Dimmable 12 V lighting, low-voltage accessory controls, sensor inputs,
   equipment feedback and room for future expansion.

The rear controller, hard switches and safety logic must operate without Wi-Fi,
Bluetooth or Ethernet. The touchscreen uses BLE as its primary convenience
interface; losing that link must not change output state or disable local rear
control.


2. INSTALLATION CONTEXT
=======================

Nominal DC system: 12 V camper/automotive electrical system.

Battery bank: three 12 V 300 Ah LiFePO4 batteries. The current assumption is
three batteries in parallel, for 12 V nominal and 900 Ah total. Confirm the
actual series/parallel wiring and the battery manufacturer's allowed voltage
range before fixing any PCB input-voltage rating.

Victron equipment currently planned/installed:

- Cerbo GX
- SmartShunt 500 A
- SmartSolar MPPT 150/45
- Orion XS 12/12-50 A DC/DC charger (confirm exact model name)

The rear cabinet will also contain or interface with:

- front and rear 12 V pot-light circuits
- front and rear RGBW ambiance-light circuits
- 12 V feeds for local USB converters
- water-pump control
- external 120 V contactors for outlets and water heater
- Dometic equipment, if its exact model supports RV-C
- cabin temperature/humidity and camper pitch/roll sensors


3. RECOMMENDED REV-A PCB SCOPE
=============================

Preferred architecture: a modular rear carrier board with replaceable modules
and field-serviceable connectors. Use an ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1-N8R8 on female
headers for Rev A rather than placing a bare ESP32-S3 module directly on the
PCB. This makes early hardware repair and firmware recovery easier. Flux may
also propose a later Rev B using an ESP32-S3-WROOM module, but it must be a
separate option and must include the antenna keepout and USB/programming design.

Rev A should contain or provide footprints/connectors for:

- protected 12 V power entry and regulated logic power
- replaceable ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1-N8R8
- one shared 3.3 V I2C expansion bus
- PCA9685 16-channel PWM controller
- MCP23017 16-bit slow digital I/O expander
- protected low-side PWM MOSFET lighting channels
- protected low-voltage relay/contactor coil drivers
- dry-contact/protected digital inputs and output-feedback inputs
- headers for the already-ordered Waveshare-compatible two-channel isolated
  CAN board: one RV-C channel and one Victron VE.Can channel
- rear microSD interface for history/logging, if reliable coexistence with the
  communications interfaces is demonstrated
- service connectors, test points, status indicators, BOOT and RESET access

For safety and thermal serviceability, Flux should also show an alternate
two-board design:

- logic/communications controller PCB
- fused high-current lighting/coil-output daughterboard

Do not combine high-current load paths with sensitive logic merely to reduce
board count. Provide the thermal and current analysis before choosing the
single-board implementation.


4. COMMUNICATION NETWORKS
=========================

There are exactly two distinct CAN networks.

A. RV-C CAN NETWORK

This network is shared by the rear BlueSquid controller, future BlueSquid nodes
and compatible RV-C equipment. The front touchscreen is not connected to it.

- Rear interface: channel 1 of the isolated dual-CAN board
- Expected bit rate: 250 kbit/s, subject to verification against the RV-C devices
- Physical topology: one linear trunk with short stubs, not a star
- Termination: exactly one 120-ohm terminator at each physical end of the
  complete shared network
- Cable: 120-ohm CAN twisted pair, with a separate power pair if one combined
  cable is used
- Connector signals: CAN_H, CAN_L, isolated bus reference and optional shield

The Waveshare touchscreen's onboard CAN transceiver remains unused and is a
future service fallback. The rear controller connects through one isolated
channel of the ordered dual-CAN board.

Using the RV-C network imposes protocol requirements:

- BlueSquid message identifiers must be selected so they do not collide with
  any RV-C PGNs or source addresses
- firmware must follow RV-C addressing, arbitration and bus-load requirements
- startup should be listen-only until the installed RV-C traffic is identified
- malformed or excessive BlueSquid traffic must not interfere with appliance
  control
- loss or reset of the rear BlueSquid node must not hold the bus dominant
- exact Dometic model, connector, bit rate, PGNs and safe fallback behaviour
  must be verified before RV-C control is enabled

B. VICTRON VE.CAN NETWORK

Channel 2 of the dual-CAN board is dedicated to the Cerbo GX VE.Can network.
It must remain electrically isolated from the RV-C network.

- rear interface: channel 2 of the isolated dual-CAN board
- expected bit rate: 250 kbit/s for the selected Cerbo VE.Can profile; confirm
  the configured Cerbo port/profile before firmware and termination sign-off
- provide ESD/transient protection and galvanic isolation
- provide selectable 120-ohm termination; enable it only when BlueSquid is at
  a physical end of the VE.Can segment
- use a passive verified Victron RJ45 pigtail/connector and clearly label it
  "VE.CAN - NOT ETHERNET"

Relevant Victron RJ45 signals:

- pin 7: CAN-H
- pin 8: CAN-L
- pin 3: NET-C/reference
- pin 6: NET-S supply; do not connect NET-S to ESP32 5 V or 3.3 V rails

BlueSquid is initially a receive-only application client for Cerbo energy data.
It must identify the correct battery, MPPT and DC/DC sources/instances and mark
values stale if the VE.Can link is lost.

C. OPTIONAL DEVELOPMENT/SERVICE RS-485

GPIO1 RX and GPIO2 TX may be preserved on an optional service header for the
existing 115200-baud development bridge. RS-485 is not the production Victron
interface. If fitted, use an automatic-direction isolated RS-485 circuit. This
header must not interfere with either production CAN network.

D. DUAL EXTERNAL CAN MODULE INTERFACE

The ordered Waveshare-compatible two-channel isolated CAN board is expected to
use two MCP2515 controllers sharing SPI. Confirm its actual schematic, crystal
frequency, isolation topology, transceivers, logic-voltage jumper and connector
pinout before producing the carrier footprint.

Provisional rear ESP32-S3 allocation:

- GPIO10: external CAN SPI SCK
- GPIO11: external CAN SPI MISO
- GPIO12: external CAN SPI MOSI
- GPIO13: RV-C MCP2515 chip select
- GPIO14: Victron VE.Can MCP2515 chip select
- GPIO15: RV-C MCP2515 interrupt
- GPIO16: Victron VE.Can MCP2515 interrupt

Use 3.3 V logic. The ordered board may require regulated 5 V power while its
logic-voltage selector is set to 3.3 V. Do not copy this assumption into the
schematic until the actual module is verified.


5. FRONT TOUCHSCREEN CONNECTION
================================

Front device: Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-7, 7-inch capacitive touchscreen.

No communications cable is required between front and rear. Run only the
appropriately fused supply cable for the touchscreen's local protected 5 V
converter. Optional service pads may expose the Waveshare CAN/RS-485 signals,
but they are not part of the installed control path.

The touchscreen must receive protected regulated 5 V, not raw camper 12 V.
Prefer separate protected front and rear DC/DC supplies so one node cannot take
down the other. Final touchscreen current requirement and converter headroom
must be taken from the exact Waveshare board documentation and measured under
full backlight/SD/BLE load.

Legacy source can use the touchscreen's onboard RS-485 for service testing:

- touchscreen RS-485 RX: GPIO16
- touchscreen RS-485 TX: GPIO15
- rear RS-485 RX: GPIO1
- rear RS-485 TX: GPIO2
- 115200 baud

This connection is disabled in the production build.


6. PRODUCTION GPIO AND BUS PLAN
==============================

The following is the preferred production allocation:

- GPIO8: shared rear I2C SDA
- GPIO9: shared rear I2C SCL
- GPIO10: dual-CAN-board SPI SCK
- GPIO11: dual-CAN-board SPI MISO
- GPIO12: dual-CAN-board SPI MOSI
- GPIO13: RV-C CAN controller CS
- GPIO14: Victron VE.Can controller CS
- GPIO15: RV-C CAN interrupt
- GPIO16: Victron VE.Can interrupt
- GPIO4: rear microSD SCK on separate SPI bus
- GPIO5: rear microSD MISO
- GPIO6: rear microSD MOSI
- GPIO7: rear microSD CS
- GPIO1/GPIO2: optional isolated RS-485/service UART fallback
- remaining verified non-strapping GPIO: enables, interrupts and service input

This allocation is provisional. Flux must check the exact DevKitC header map,
boot-strapping pins, USB/JTAG use, PSRAM/flash restrictions and input/output
capability before routing.

CURRENT BENCH MAP - DO NOT COPY TO THE PRODUCTION PCB:

- HTU21D uses a separate I2C bus on GPIO10/GPIO11
- GY-61/ADXL335 uses analog GPIO4/GPIO5/GPIO6
- direct RGBW prototypes use GPIO12 through GPIO18 and GPIO21
- hard all-lights button uses GPIO35

These current assignments overlap the proposed CAN and SD interfaces. The
production PCB should remove those conflicts by using the PCA9685, MCP23017,
SHT31 and LIS3DH described below.


7. I2C DEVICE PLAN
==================

Shared 3.3 V I2C bus on GPIO8/GPIO9, initially 400 kHz where all installed
devices and wiring support it. Provide correctly calculated pull-ups and do not
populate duplicate strong pull-ups on every module.

Planned addresses:

- LIS3DH pitch/roll accelerometer: 0x18
- MCP23017 digital I/O expander: 0x20
- PCA9685 PWM controller: 0x40
- SHT31 temperature/humidity sensor: 0x44
- optional ADS1115 ADC: 0x48
- optional DS3231 RTC: 0x68

Use SHT31 instead of HTU21D because HTU21D conflicts with PCA9685 at address
0x40. Use LIS3DH instead of the analog GY-61/ADXL335 to reduce analog wiring and
free GPIO. The cabin sensor should be remote from warm power electronics; give
it a keyed low-voltage connector rather than measuring PCB enclosure air.

Expose one expansion I2C connector with 3V3, GND, SDA and SCL. This connector
is for short, local protected wiring only, not a long camper-wide bus.


8. LIGHTING OUTPUTS
===================

Current functional requirement:

- front pot lights: one independently dimmable white 12 V circuit
- rear pot lights: one independently dimmable white 12 V circuit
- front ambiance light: one independently controlled common-positive RGBW zone
- rear ambiance light: one independently controlled common-positive RGBW zone
- spare dimmable channels for expansion

Minimum active channel count is 10: two white channels plus eight RGBW
channels. Provide at least 12 protected channels; using all 16 PCA9685 channels
is preferred if board area and thermal capacity permit.

Use generic PCB and connector names so firmware-configured labels can change:

- PWM_OUT_1 through PWM_OUT_16
- RGBW grouping may be shown in documentation, but silkscreen must not lock a
  channel to a room name

Electrical requirements:

- PCA9685 creates PWM logic only and must never drive LED loads directly
- 12 V common-positive LED loads use protected low-side N-channel switching
- select MOSFETs that are fully enhanced by the implemented gate-drive voltage
- do not assume an arbitrary "logic-level" MOSFET is suitable; verify RDS(on)
  at the actual gate voltage and hot junction temperature
- PCA9685 outputs are open-drain; design the pull-up/gate-driver network so
  every MOSFET is positively off during reset, unpowered states and I2C faults
- include gate resistance and gate-to-source pulldown/off bias
- outputs must default OFF at boot and while the ESP32 is held in reset
- target PWM frequency is approximately 1 kHz unless EMC/acoustic testing
  indicates another value
- provide branch-current test points and enough copper/thermal vias
- keep high-current LED returns out of the ESP32/sensor ground paths
- run both positive and switched-negative conductors to each load
- do not use vehicle chassis as the switched RGBW return
- size each RGBW common-positive conductor for the sum of all four channels

Preliminary development limit is approximately 2 A continuous per channel,
but this is NOT a final PCB rating. Flux must request/receive the real maximum
current, cable length, wire gauge, fuse value and duty cycle for every output
before selecting MOSFETs, connectors, copper weight and thermal design.

Every lighting branch must be separately fused. Preferred Rev-A arrangement is
an external automotive fuse block close to the supply, unless the PCB includes
properly rated, accessible and replaceable fuse holders.


9. ACCESSORY OUTPUTS
====================

Use generic names such as ACC_OUT_1 through ACC_OUT_8. User-visible labels and
icons are configured in software and exported/imported through the touchscreen.

Planned functions include USB power enables, water pump and external contactor
coils. These functions do not all use the same power stage.

A. LOW-VOLTAGE COIL OUTPUTS

- MCP23017 provides slow logic I/O
- ULN2803A or individually selected protected drivers sink 12 V relay/contactor
  coils
- if ULN2803A is used, connect its COM/flyback-diode node correctly to the coil
  supply and verify coil current and dissipation
- provide OFF-at-reset bias independent of firmware
- provide optional feedback input for important commanded loads
- exact number, coil voltage and coil current are TBD

B. USB OUTLETS

Switch a fused 12 V feed to a local automotive USB converter at the outlet.
Do not distribute 5 V from the rear PCB through the camper. A nominal 60 W USB
outlet can draw roughly 5 A or more from 12 V after losses; the PCB output may
therefore need to control an external relay/high-side switch rather than carry
the outlet current. Exact outlet count and power are TBD.

C. WATER PUMP

The pump is a motor with significant start/stall current. Do not connect it to
a lighting MOSFET channel. Drive a correctly rated automotive relay/contactor
or protected high-side motor switch. Preserve a physical manual override and
make the default restart state OFF. Pump model, running current, stall/inrush
current and fuse are TBD.

D. 120 V OUTLETS AND WATER HEATER

Never route 120 VAC onto this PCB. The PCB may drive only an isolated protected
12 V coil of an external, code-appropriate two-pole contactor. Mains wiring,
breaker/GFCI and contactor contacts remain in a separate approved enclosure.
The water heater's thermostat and thermal cutoff remain functional. Provide a
physical OFF/AUTO or OFF/AUTO/ON selector and a safe OFF reset state.


10. INPUTS AND FEEDBACK
=======================

Provide generic DIN_1 through DIN_16 names using MCP23017 and/or protected
native inputs. Expected uses include momentary hard switches, relay auxiliary
contacts, pump status, alarm contacts and future expansion.

Dry-contact input requirements:

- 3.3 V logic
- defined pull-up/pull-down
- series current limiting
- RC filtering/debounce
- ESD/transient protection
- keyed signal-and-ground connector
- no floating connector pins

Do not accept raw vehicle 12 V on a dry-contact input. If 12 V sense inputs are
desired, create a separately labelled protected 12V_DIN circuit using an
automotive-rated divider/comparator, optocoupler or digital-input IC. Define
its off/on thresholds, reverse-voltage survival and transient rating.

At least one entry/emergency light should retain a direct hard-wired manual
fallback that does not depend on the touchscreen, CAN network or firmware.

Front local controls are expected to use an ADS1115 behind the touchscreen:

- A0: resistor ladder for momentary switches
- A1: PIR motion sensor
- A2: ambient-light sensor divider
- A3: spare

This front ADS1115 normally belongs near the touchscreen and is not part of the
rear PCB. Its inputs must remain between 0 V and 3.3 V.


11. POWER ENTRY AND PROTECTION
==============================

The PCB is connected to a noisy nominal-12 V RV/vehicle environment. The input
stage must include or explicitly require:

- correctly sized source fuse close to the battery/distribution bus
- reverse-polarity protection
- over-current protection
- surge/load-dump/transient suppression appropriate to the selected design
  standard
- undervoltage and overvoltage behaviour that leaves outputs safe
- input and regulator filtering for CAN, sensors and ESP32 stability
- brownout handling and watchdog recovery

Do not choose a maximum input voltage from the phrase "12 V". Confirm battery
charge voltage and the vehicle transient standard/test pulse that Rev A must
survive. Flux should propose the protection components and show their energy,
voltage and temperature margins.

Preferred prototype power arrangement:

- protected external automotive/RV 12 V to 5 V converter module for the rear
  controller, connected through a keyed power connector
- separate protected 12 V to 5 V supply for the front touchscreen
- local low-noise 3.3 V regulation/decoupling as required by the selected
  controller/modules

An isolated 5 V module such as the Mean Well RSD/DDR class may be evaluated,
but no exact converter is approved yet. Produce a power budget for ESP32 peak
load, CAN modules, SD card, sensors, PWM logic, relay coils and indicator LEDs.

Separate these current paths in placement/routing:

- logic and sensors
- CAN/RS-485 interfaces
- relay/contactor coils
- LED load currents

Use deliberate star/partitioned returns so LED and coil current does not flow
through sensor or ESP32 ground paths. Preserve galvanic-isolation boundaries;
do not bridge them with copper pours, shields or programming cables without an
explicit design decision.


12. CONNECTORS AND SERVICEABILITY
=================================

Preferred connector policy:

- pluggable, touch-safe terminal blocks for field power and load wiring
- keyed locking connectors for sensors and low-current signals
- ferrule-compatible terminals for stranded camper wiring
- separate connectors for each CAN network to prevent accidental cross-connection
- connector current/temperature rating must exceed the protected circuit rating
- no unkeyed identical connector should make it easy to apply 12 V to 3.3 V I/O

Required service features:

- accessible ESP32 USB programming connector
- accessible BOOT and RESET controls
- labelled 3V3, 5V, 12V, GND and isolated-ground test points
- SDA/SCL, SPI, UART and CAN TX/RX test points
- CAN_H/CAN_L test points on every isolated bus side
- status LEDs that can be disabled for low-power operation
- mounting holes and mechanical keepouts
- replaceable controller and communications modules where practical
- clear pin-1, polarity, fuse and termination markings

All field channel names should remain generic on the PCB. Room/device names are
assigned by the touchscreen configuration manager and included in SD-card
configuration export/import.


13. PCB LAYOUT REQUIREMENTS
===========================

Flux should propose stackup after completing the current and EMC analysis.
Preferred starting point is four layers for continuous reference planes and
clean signal routing. If significant lighting current is routed on-board,
evaluate 2 oz outer copper, connector heating, trace/via temperature rise and
fault current. Keeping high-current switching on a daughterboard may be safer
and easier to service.

Layout constraints:

- ESP32 antenna at a board edge with the full manufacturer keepout: no copper,
  traces, components, enclosure metal or cable bundles in the keepout
- keep switch-node, MOSFET, LED and coil paths away from sensors, antenna and
  communication clocks
- short local decoupling at every IC and module connector
- controlled, closely coupled CAN differential routing on the PCB
- TVS/ESD parts adjacent to external connectors
- isolation creepage/clearance per the selected isolator, working voltage and
  pollution environment
- no 120 V creepage zone is required because no mains may enter this PCB
- thermal vias and copper spreading for power devices, backed by calculation
- avoid routing high-current returns through logic ground necks
- allow enclosure airflow and service access to terminal screws/fuses
- provide silkscreen readable after modules are installed


14. FIRMWARE AND FAIL-SAFE BEHAVIOUR
====================================

Firmware platform: Arduino framework on ESP32-S3 using PlatformIO.

Relevant current behaviour:

- rear controller owns physical outputs and continues safe operation without
  the touchscreen
- labels/icons are configurable and therefore must not be hard-coded into PCB
  channel names
- device state/settings are persisted in controller nonvolatile storage
- touchscreen can export/import the current configuration using its SD card
- screen sleep and communications loss must not change output state unexpectedly
- BLE application protocol has sequence-numbered commands, acknowledgements,
  state revisions and online status

Hardware must support these fail-safe rules even before firmware starts:

- lighting, pump and contactor commands default OFF
- no momentary boot pulse on a gate or relay driver
- MCU reset or unplugging a module cannot directly energize a load
- mains contactors require an explicit valid command and retain independent
  appliance safety devices/manual disconnects
- use watchdog and brownout reset
- include an output-enable strategy if it improves deterministic startup safety


15. ITEMS THAT MUST BE CONFIRMED BEFORE SCHEMATIC FREEZE
========================================================

1. Exact rear ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 board revision and header dimensions.
2. Whether Rev A is one combined board or logic plus power-output daughterboard.
3. Exact current, fuse, conductor and cable length for every light channel.
4. Exact RGBW strip/pot-light electrical topology and common-positive confirmation.
5. Exact count/current of USB outlets, relays, contactors and feedback inputs.
6. Water-pump model, running current, start/stall current and manual override.
7. 12 V input operating range and automotive/RV transient test requirement.
8. Selected 5 V converter(s) and measured front/rear peak power.
9. Exact ordered dual isolated CAN board schematic, pinout and dimensions.
10. Exact Dometic appliance/controller model, RV-C connector and supported messages.
11. RV-C identifiers, addressing and measured bus load.
12. Termination locations for the RV-C bus and VE.Can bus.
13. Exact Cerbo GX model, VE.Can port/profile, bit rate and published sources.
14. Enclosure dimensions, mounting pattern, ambient temperature and airflow.
15. Preferred field connector family and available wire gauges.
16. Whether rear SD logging remains on Rev A and required card connector type.


16. REQUESTED FLUX.AI OUTPUTS
=============================

Please produce the following in reviewable stages. Do not proceed directly to
manufacturing files without resolving the TBD list.

Stage 1 - architecture review

- system block diagram with all isolation and power domains
- single-board and two-board architecture comparison
- preliminary power/current/thermal budget
- GPIO, peripheral and connector allocation table
- conflict check against ESP32-S3 boot/USB/PSRAM constraints
- list of assumptions and unresolved safety/rating questions

Stage 2 - schematic

- hierarchical sheets for power/protection, ESP32 carrier, I2C devices,
  communications, PWM outputs, coil outputs, digital inputs and connectors
- manufacturer part numbers, not generic unlabeled parts
- component voltage/current/temperature derating notes
- selectable CAN termination and visible isolation boundaries
- test points and safe reset biasing
- ERC report and design-review checklist

Stage 3 - PCB

- board outline coordinated with enclosure and service access
- proposed stackup and copper weight
- isolation, antenna and high-current keepouts
- trace/via current and temperature-rise calculations
- thermal analysis for worst-case simultaneous outputs
- EMI/ESD review for the RV environment
- DRC report and 3D assembly review

Stage 4 - manufacturing package after approval

- Gerbers and drill files
- pick-and-place/centroid file
- BOM with supplier/manufacturer part numbers and alternates
- assembly drawings
- connector/pinout and wiring document
- bring-up/test procedure and test-point table
- revision-controlled schematic and PCB source


17. DESIGN PRIORITIES
=====================

In order of importance:

1. Electrical and fire safety.
2. Fail-safe behaviour and manual fallback for critical functions.
3. Safe RV-C coexistence and isolation between RV-C and VE.Can.
4. Serviceability and clear field wiring.
5. Rear control and safe output behaviour without Wi-Fi/Bluetooth/Ethernet.
6. Expandability through generic channels and buses.
7. Compact size, provided it does not compromise heat, isolation or repair.
8. Cost.
